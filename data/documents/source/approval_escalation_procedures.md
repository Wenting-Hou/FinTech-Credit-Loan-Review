# Approval / Escalation Procedures

**Policy ID:** APPR-001  
**Version:** V1.0  
**Status:** Effective  
**Effective date:** January 1, 2024  
**Prepared:** December 15, 2023   
**Proposed owner:** Credit Risk Function    
**Proposed approver:** Credit Risk Committee  
**Review cycle:** Annually

---

## Purpose

Describes the standard approval authority levels and escalation paths for applications across **Mortgage**, **Credit Card**, and **Auto**, so that loan officers/underwriters know when a decision can be made at their level versus when it must be routed upward.

---

## 1. General Escalation Principles

1. **Automated/system decision first.** Most applications within standard product criteria are decisioned by an automated underwriting system (AUS) or credit decision engine.
2. **Exceptions require human review.** Anything outside standard guidelines that is not a catalog gate (for example LTV, income documentation, reserves or property issues) triggers manual underwriter review.
3. **Escalation is tiered by risk/exposure**, not just by dollar amount. Higher-risk products (e.g., Subprime, FHA with compensating factors) escalate faster than lower-risk ones even at similar dollar amounts.
4. **Every escalation must be documented** with the specific exception reason and the compensating factor(s) considered.
5. **Escalation never overrides a failed catalog gate.** An application that fails any gate in the Catalog Gates table of ELIG-001 (minimum risk tier, minimum FICO, maximum payment DTI, maximum amount, bankruptcy, collections) is not eligible for that product. It is declined or offered another product; no approval level in this document can waive it. The exceptions below apply only to requirements outside those gates.
6. **Applicant risk tier routing applies first.** T4 applicants go to the senior product reviewer and T5 applicants go to the Credit Risk Committee, as set out in PRODUCT-RISK section 3. They are never decided by the automated engine alone.

## 2. Mortgage Approval Levels

| Level | Authority | Typical Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **AUS Approve/Eligible** | System-level | Standard FHA/Conventional within guideline; no manual review needed beyond doc verification |
| **Underwriter I** | Individual underwriter | Conventional/FHA with minor exceptions outside the catalog gates (e.g., LTV slightly above guideline with extra reserves) |
| **Senior Underwriter / Underwriting Manager** | Team lead | Jumbo loans, LTV exceptions beyond Underwriter I authority, self-employed income calculations requiring judgment |
| **Credit Risk Committee** | Committee | Loans with multiple layered risk factors (e.g., high LTV + high DTI + non-traditional credit), any loan requiring policy exception sign-off |
| **Chief Credit Officer / Board-level** | Executive | Large jumbo exposures above internal concentration limits, reputational-risk loans |

**Escalation triggers (Mortgage):**
- AUS "Refer" or "Ineligible" decision
- LTV outside standard guideline (a DTI above the product's catalog maximum is a decline, not an escalation)
- Non-traditional credit history (thin file)
- Property flags (flood zone issues, condo non-warrantable status, unique property type)
- Any indication of fraud (undisclosed debt, income inconsistency)

---

## 3. Credit Card Approval Levels

| Level | Authority | Typical Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Automated Decision Engine** | System-level | Secured and Standard card applications that pass every catalog gate, for T1 to T3 applicants |
| **Credit Analyst** | Individual | Standard card borderline cases that pass every catalog gate (e.g., thin file or income documentation questions) |
| **Underwriting Supervisor** | Team lead | Elite card approvals above the analyst's credit-limit authority (still within the catalog maximum), or Standard card exceptions outside the catalog gates |
| **Credit Risk Committee** | Committee | Credit limit increases on existing accounts, high-exposure Elite card accounts, policy exception requests outside the catalog gates |

**Escalation triggers (Credit Card):**
- Score at or just above the product minimum with other weak factors (a score below the minimum is a decline, not an escalation)
- Requested limit above the analyst's authority but within the catalog maximum
- Recent derogatory marks (charge-off, collection) within lookback window
- Suspected synthetic identity or first-party fraud indicators

---

## 4. Auto Loan Approval Levels

| Level | Authority | Typical Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Automated Decision Engine** | System-level | Top-tier and Traditional applications that pass every catalog gate and are within standard LTV and term guidelines |
| **Loan Officer** | Individual | Traditional/Subprime with minor stipulation exceptions (e.g., slightly elevated LTV with larger down payment) |
| **Underwriting Manager** | Team lead | Subprime approvals above standard authority, term/LTV exceptions |
| **Credit Risk Committee** | Committee | Portfolio concentration exceptions (e.g., too much subprime volume in a period), dealer-relationship policy exceptions |

**Escalation triggers (Auto):**
- LTV above the product maximum (including add-ons/GAP)
- DTI close to the product maximum (a DTI above the catalog maximum is a decline, not an escalation)
- Vehicle outside standard age/mileage eligibility
- Indirect/dealer-sourced loan with stipulation waivers requested

---

## 5. Cross-Product Escalation Rules

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Suspected fraud (any product) | Immediate escalation to Fraud/BSA-AML team; freeze application pending review — **do not proceed with standard underwriting escalation path** |
| OFAC/sanctions match | Immediate escalation per BSA/AML procedures; do not disclose match reason to applicant |
| Applicant complaint tied to a declined application | Escalate to Compliance for adverse action notice (ECOA) accuracy review |
| Policy exception requested outside any defined authority level | Escalate to Credit Risk Committee with full documentation package |

---

## 6. Documentation Requirements for Any Escalation

1. Application summary (product, applicant risk tier, key metrics: score, DTI, LTV/limit)
2. Reason for escalation (which guideline was not met)
3. Compensating factors considered (reserves, tenure, relationship history, etc.)
4. Recommendation from the escalating party
5. Final decision and decisioning authority name/level
6. Date/timestamp for audit trail

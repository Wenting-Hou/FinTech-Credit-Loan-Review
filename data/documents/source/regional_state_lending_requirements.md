## Purpose

This document summarizes the regional and state-level regulatory considerations that apply to loan origination and servicing across the three product lines: **Mortgage**, **Credit Card**, and **Auto**. It is intended as a reference for loan officers, underwriters, and compliance staff to understand how location-based rules affect eligibility, disclosures, and pricing.

---

## 1. General Framework

Lending is governed by a layered set of requirements:

1. **Federal law** — sets the floor (e.g., Truth in Lending Act (TILA), Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA), Fair Housing Act, Military Lending Act (MLA)).
2. **State law** — can impose stricter limits on top of federal law (usury caps, licensing, disclosure timing, cooling-off periods).
3. **Local/municipal rules** — occasionally add requirements in specific cities/counties (e.g., predatory lending ordinances, foreclosure mediation programs).

**Rule of thumb:** Where state law is stricter than federal law, the state law governs (except where federal law explicitly preempts it, common for federally chartered banks).

## 2. Mortgage — Regional Considerations

| Area | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Licensing** | Loan originators must be licensed in every state where they originate loans (NMLS registration via the SAFE Act). |
| **Usury limits** | Most states exempt first-lien mortgages from usury caps, but second liens/HELOCs may be capped in some states. |
| **High-cost/HOEPA thresholds** | Some states set high-cost mortgage triggers stricter than federal HOEPA thresholds (e.g., NY, MA, NC). |
| **Foreclosure process** | Judicial foreclosure states (e.g., NY, FL, IL) require court involvement; non-judicial states (e.g., TX, CA, GA) allow faster foreclosure via power of sale. |
| **Escrow requirements** | Some states mandate escrow accounts for taxes/insurance regardless of LTV; others follow federal HPML rules only. |
| **Prepayment penalties** | Prohibited or restricted in many states even where federal law allows them for certain loan types. |
| **Conforming loan limits** | FHFA sets county-level conforming loan limits annually; high-cost counties (e.g., parts of CA, NY, HI) have higher limits than the baseline. |
| **FHA geographic limits** | FHA loan limits also vary by county based on local median home prices. |

### Tier-specific notes
- **FHA:** County loan limits apply; upfront and annual mortgage insurance premium (MIP) is federally set but property eligibility can vary by state (e.g., flood zone, condo approval rules).
- **Conventional:** Conforming loan limits vary by county; state-specific escrow and disclosure timing rules apply.
- **Jumbo:** No federal loan-limit cap, but state usury/disclosure rules and county property valuation norms still apply; some states require additional high-net-worth borrower disclosures.

## 3. Credit Card — Regional Considerations

| Area | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Usury/interest rate caps** | Federally chartered banks can "export" the interest rate of their home state nationwide (per *Marquette* doctrine), effectively bypassing borrower-state usury caps. |
| **Fee limits** | Some states restrict late fees, annual fees, or over-limit fees beyond federal Reg Z / CARD Act baselines. |
| **Disclosure language** | A few states require additional or bilingual disclosures (e.g., California, Texas for Spanish-language markets). |
| **Data/privacy law** | State privacy laws (e.g., California Consumer Privacy Act) affect how card issuers can use applicant data for underwriting/marketing. |
| **Military/MLA** | 36% APR cap and additional protections apply nationwide to active-duty servicemembers and dependents, regardless of state. |

### Tier-specific notes
- **Secured:** Security deposit handling (escrow, FDIC-insured account) may be subject to state deposit account rules.
- **Standard:** Standard Reg Z / CARD Act disclosures apply nationwide; state add-ons mostly relate to fee caps.
- **Elite (720+):** Rewards/points programs may trigger state-specific consumer protection or gift-card/loyalty-point escheatment rules if unredeemed for long periods.

## 4. Auto Loan — Regional Considerations

| Area | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Usury limits** | Auto loan interest rate caps vary significantly by state; some states have no cap for licensed lenders, others cap in the 18–36% APR range depending on loan size. |
| **Title/lien perfection** | Process for recording a lien on a vehicle title differs by state (electronic lien and title (ELT) states vs. paper title states). |
| **Repossession rules** | "Right to cure" periods, repossession notice requirements, and post-repo sale procedures vary by state. |
| **Dealer licensing** | Indirect lending (dealer-originated loans purchased by a bank) requires the dealer to be licensed per state motor vehicle dealer laws. |
| **GAP insurance/add-ons** | Some states cap or regulate GAP waiver and add-on product pricing. |
| **Sales tax/registration** | State and local sales tax on vehicle purchase affects amount financed and loan-to-value calculations. |

### Tier-specific notes
- **Subprime:** Higher scrutiny in states with strict repossession/right-to-cure laws; add-on product regulation is especially relevant given higher attach rates in this tier.
- **Traditional:** Standard state usury and title-perfection rules apply.
- **Top-tier credit:** Rate is typically well under state usury caps; primary regional factor is title/registration processing time.
